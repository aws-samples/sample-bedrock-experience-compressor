# RB-THERMAL-001: Heat Exchanger Tube Bundle Cleaning

**Procedure Code:** PROC-HX-2024-001
**Runbook ID:** RB-THERMAL-001
**Version:** 2.0
**Date:** 2024-03-25
**Classification:** Preventive Maintenance - Confined Space

## 1. Objective
Bi-annual cleaning of shell-and-tube heat exchanger tube bundles to remove scale, fouling, and deposits that reduce heat transfer efficiency.

## 2. Prerequisites
- Heat exchanger isolated from process (both shell side and tube side)
- System depressurized and drained
- Lockout/tagout applied per LOTO-HX-001
- Confined space entry permit obtained
- Chemical cleaning tank available and tested
- Tube bundle extraction equipment staged
- Gasket replacement kit verified in stock
- Disposal contractor confirmed for cleaning chemicals

## 3. Required Tools
- Tube bundle extraction trolley (rails and winch system)
- Chain hoist (2-5 tonne capacity depending on bundle size)
- Chemical cleaning tank (sized for tube bundle immersion)
- Pressure test pump (hydraulic, 0-50 bar range)
- Tube plugging kit (plugs, expander tool, leak sealant)
- Torque wrench 100-500 Nm (calibrated)
- Gasket material (consult heat exchanger specification sheet)
- PPE (chemical-resistant suit, gloves, face shield, respirator)

## 4. Estimated Duration
**6 hours** (typical shell-and-tube heat exchanger, 200-400 tubes)

## 5. Safety Notes
⚠️ **CRITICAL SAFETY WARNINGS:**
- Confined space hazards - tube bundle extraction requires entry into shell
- Chemical hazards - cleaning solution acidic or caustic (pH 2-3 or pH 11-12)
- Heavy lifting - tube bundle mass typically 500-2000 kg
- Hot surfaces - allow minimum 4 hours cooldown before opening
- Pressure hazard during leak test - stand clear of flanges during pressurization
- Disposal of cleaning chemicals requires environmental permit

## STEP-BY-STEP PROCEDURE

### Step 1: Isolation and Depressurization (30 min)

**Actions:**
1. Verify heat exchanger isolated from process:
   - Shell side inlet valve closed and locked (LOTO tag)
   - Shell side outlet valve closed and locked (LOTO tag)
   - Tube side inlet valve closed and locked (LOTO tag)
   - Tube side outlet valve closed and locked (LOTO tag)
2. Verify zero pressure on both sides using pressure gauges
3. Open drain valves on shell side and tube side
4. Allow complete drainage (expect 50-200 liters depending on heat exchanger size)
5. Verify temperature <40°C using thermal camera (minimum 4 hours cooldown)
6. Open vent valves to atmospheric pressure
7. Apply lockout tags per Form LOTO-HX-001 (4 isolation valves + 2 drain valves)
8. Post warning signs on heat exchanger

**Isolation Verification:**
- Shell side pressure: 0 bar (verified)
- Tube side pressure: 0 bar (verified)
- Temperature: <40°C (verified)
- Drainage complete: yes/no

**Tools:**
- Pressure gauges (on heat exchanger)
- Thermal camera
- Drain containers (for fluid collection)
- LOTO locks and tags

**Safety:**
⚠️ **CRITICAL:** Do not open flanges until pressure zero and temperature <40°C
⚠️ Verify LOTO tags on all isolation valves before proceeding

**Expected Result:**
- Heat exchanger fully isolated and depressurized
- Temperature safe for personnel (<40°C)
- Drained and vented
- LOTO applied and documented

---

### Step 2: Header Removal (60 min)

**Actions:**
1. Position drip trays under both tube sheet headers (channel heads)
2. Remove insulation from headers (may contain asbestos - verify material first)
3. Identify header bolts (typical configuration: 16-24 M20 bolts per header)
4. Mark bolt positions with numbered tags (for tracking and reinstallation)
5. Remove header bolts in cross pattern:
   - Start at top center
   - Work in cross pattern to prevent binding
   - Store bolts in numbered tray with anti-seize applied
6. Inspect bolt threads for damage (galling, corrosion, stripping)
7. Install lifting eyes on headers (if not permanently installed)
8. Connect chain hoist to overhead beam
9. Lift headers carefully (weight typically 100-300 kg each)
10. Store headers on padded supports (prevent damage to machined faces)
11. Remove tube sheet gaskets (inspect condition, measure compression)
12. Store gaskets for comparison (replaced during reassembly)

**Bolt Removal Sequence (Cross Pattern):**
```
For typical round header with 20 bolts:
Start at 12 o'clock position, alternate across diameter:
1→11→6→16→3→13→8→18→5→15→10→20→2→12→7→17→4→14→9→19
Prevents uneven stress and binding during removal
```

**Tools:**
- Socket wrench (30mm typical for M20 bolts)
- Impact wrench (optional, if bolts not overly corroded)
- Chain hoist (1-2 tonne capacity for headers)
- Lifting eyes (M20 thread typical)
- Numbered bolt storage trays
- Anti-seize compound
- Padded supports for header storage

**Safety:**
⚠️ Headers heavy (100-300 kg) - use proper lifting equipment
⚠️ Expect residual fluid release when opening - wear face shield

**Expected Result:**
- Both headers removed and stored safely
- Bolts removed, inspected, and stored with tracking
- Gaskets removed for replacement
- Tube sheet exposed for bundle extraction

**Common Issues:**
- Header bolts often corroded - penetrating oil may be required
- Gaskets stick to tube sheet - careful removal prevents damage to machined surface

---

### Step 3: Tube Bundle Extraction (45 min)

**Actions:**
1. Install tube bundle extraction rails (if not permanent installation):
   - Rails extend from tube sheet to outside heat exchanger
   - Typically 3-4 meters length depending on tube length
2. Position extraction trolley at tube sheet
3. Connect trolley to tube bundle lifting lugs (typically 4 lugs welded to tube sheet)
4. Verify winch and trolley rated for bundle weight (typically 500-2000 kg)
5. Begin winch operation:
   - Pull slowly and steadily (5-10 cm per minute)
   - Watch for binding or resistance (may indicate tube fouling or misalignment)
   - If resistance, stop and investigate (do not force)
6. Extract tube bundle completely from shell
7. Position tube bundle on extraction trolley outside heat exchanger
8. Inspect shell interior for corrosion, deposits, or damage (use flashlight)
9. Photograph shell interior for documentation

**Tube Bundle Extraction Precautions:**
- Do not force extraction - binding indicates problem
- Support bundle evenly during extraction (prevent bending)
- Watch clearances (bundle diameter typically close tolerance to shell ID)

**Tools:**
- Tube bundle extraction rails
- Extraction trolley
- Winch (manual or electric, rated for bundle weight)
- Chain fall (backup for winch)
- Flashlight for shell inspection
- Digital camera

**Safety:**
⚠️ **CRITICAL:** Tube bundle very heavy (500-2000 kg) - verify equipment rated for load
⚠️ Never walk under suspended bundle
⚠️ Extraction creates confined space in shell - do not enter without atmosphere test

**Expected Result:**
- Tube bundle extracted completely from shell
- Bundle positioned on trolley outside heat exchanger
- Shell interior inspected and documented
- No damage to bundle or shell during extraction

---

### Step 4: Chemical Cleaning (120 min)

**Actions:**
1. Transport tube bundle to chemical cleaning tank (if stationary tank available)
2. If no tank available, construct temporary containment:
   - Place bundle in large trough or tank
   - Ensure containment rated for chemical solution volume
3. Prepare chemical cleaning solution per specification:
   - For mineral scale (calcium, magnesium carbonate): acidic cleaner (pH 2-3)
     - Typical: 5-10% hydrochloric acid or citric acid solution
   - For organic fouling (biological growth, oil): alkaline cleaner (pH 11-12)
     - Typical: 2-5% sodium hydroxide solution
   - Verify chemical compatibility with tube material (carbon steel, stainless steel, copper-nickel alloy)
4. Immerse tube bundle in cleaning solution:
   - Ensure complete submersion of tube surfaces
   - Solution level above top tubes by minimum 10 cm
5. Soak time per chemical manufacturer recommendation (typically 60-90 minutes)
6. Agitate solution periodically (if circulation pump available)
7. Monitor cleaning progress visually (solution discoloration indicates dissolved deposits)
8. After soak time, remove bundle from cleaning solution
9. Rinse bundle thoroughly with fresh water (high pressure if available):
   - Flush through tubes to remove dissolved scale
   - Rinse exterior surfaces
   - Continue rinsing until rinse water runs clear
10. Inspect tube bundle after cleaning:
    - Visual inspection for remaining deposits
    - If heavy deposits remain, repeat cleaning cycle
11. Neutralize and dispose of cleaning solution per environmental regulations

**Chemical Cleaning Solution Calculations:**
```
For typical tube bundle with 300 tubes, 3 meters long, 25mm OD:
- Tube surface area ≈ 70 m²
- Solution volume required ≈ 500-1000 liters (depends on tank size)
- Chemical quantity: 5% HCl solution = 25-50 liters concentrated acid
```

**Tools:**
- Chemical cleaning tank (or temporary containment trough)
- Cleaning chemicals (acid or alkaline per specification)
- Mixing containers and measuring equipment
- pH meter (verify solution concentration)
- High-pressure water hose (for rinsing)
- PPE (chemical-resistant suit, gloves, face shield, respirator)
- Neutralizing agent (for chemical disposal)

**Safety:**
⚠️ **CRITICAL:** Cleaning chemicals corrosive - wear full PPE
⚠️ Acid and caustic fumes toxic - work in ventilated area or wear respirator
⚠️ Chemical disposal requires environmental permit - coordinate with contractor
⚠️ Neutralize spills immediately (acid spill: sodium bicarbonate; caustic spill: weak acid)

**Expected Result:**
- Tube bundle cleaned (scale and fouling removed)
- Visual inspection shows clean tube surfaces
- Bundle rinsed thoroughly (no chemical residue)
- Cleaning solution neutralized and disposed per regulations

**Common Issues:**
- Tube fouling often worse than expected - may require extended soak time or second cleaning cycle
- Chemical disposal coordination critical - verify contractor available before starting

### Step 5: Tube Bundle Reinstallation (60 min)

**Actions:**
1. Inspect tube bundle for damage after cleaning:
   - Look for corroded or damaged tubes (thin walls, pitting, cracks)
   - Perform visual inspection through tubes (use flashlight and mirror)
   - If damaged tubes found, mark for plugging
2. Position cleaned tube bundle on extraction trolley (aligned with shell)
3. Inspect shell interior one final time (remove any debris or tools)
4. Begin winch operation to insert bundle into shell:
   - Push slowly and steadily
   - Ensure alignment (watch for binding)
   - Guide bundle to prevent scraping shell interior
5. When bundle fully inserted, verify tube sheet flush with shell flange face
6. Inspect tube ends at tube sheet (verify no damage during insertion)

**Tube Plugging (if damaged tubes found):**
- Insert expanding plug into damaged tube
- Use expander tool to expand plug and seal tube
- Verify plug secure (pull test)
- Mark plugged tubes on heat exchanger datasheet
- Note: >10% tubes plugged requires engineering review (heat transfer capacity reduced)

**Tools:**
- Extraction trolley and winch
- Tube plugging kit (expanding plugs, expander tool)
- Flashlight and inspection mirror
- Alignment guides (if available)

**Safety:**
⚠️ Do not enter shell during bundle insertion (crushing hazard)
⚠️ Watch for pinch points between bundle and shell

**Expected Result:**
- Tube bundle reinstalled in shell
- Bundle aligned with tube sheet flanges
- Damaged tubes plugged (if any)
- Shell interior clean (no debris or tools left inside)

---

### Step 6: Header Reassembly (50 min)

**Actions:**
1. Inspect tube sheet surfaces (both ends):
   - Clean surfaces with solvent and lint-free cloth
   - Check for damage, corrosion, or warping
   - Verify surface flatness (use straightedge if concern)
2. Install new tube sheet gaskets:
   - Select correct gasket material per specification:
     - Compressed fiber for low-pressure (<10 bar)
     - Spiral-wound metal for high-pressure (>10 bar)
     - Rubber for water service (if compatible with temperature)
   - Position gasket on tube sheet flange (align bolt holes)
   - Verify gasket sits flat (no wrinkles or folds)
3. Lift headers into position using chain hoist
4. Align header bolt holes with tube sheet flange
5. Install header bolts finger-tight in cross pattern
6. Torque bolts to specification in 3 passes (cross pattern):
   - Pass 1: 30% of final torque (e.g., 150 Nm if final is 450 Nm)
   - Pass 2: 70% of final torque (e.g., 300 Nm)
   - Pass 3: 100% of final torque (e.g., 450 Nm)
7. Mark bolts with torque paint for verification
8. Reinstall insulation on headers (if removed)

**Torque Specifications (typical for M20 bolts):**
- Carbon steel bolts: 400-500 Nm (verify per heat exchanger specification)
- Stainless steel bolts: 350-450 Nm
- Bolt material and grade affects torque - consult specification sheet

**Torque Sequence (Cross Pattern):**
```
Same as removal sequence but in reverse:
Pass 1 (30%): 1→11→6→16→3→13→8→18→5→15→10→20→2→12→7→17→4→14→9→19
Pass 2 (70%): Same sequence
Pass 3 (100%): Same sequence
```

**Tools:**
- Torque wrench 100-500 Nm (calibrated <6 months)
- New gasket sets (per specification)
- Solvent and cleaning cloths
- Chain hoist (for header lifting)
- Torque paint marker

**Safety:**
⚠️ Use calibrated torque wrench only (over-torque damages gaskets, under-torque causes leaks)
⚠️ Header heavy - use chain hoist, do not attempt manual lift

**Expected Result:**
- New gaskets installed
- Headers bolted and torqued to specification
- Torque paint marks visible
- Ready for pressure test

**Common Issues:**
- Gasket availability often problematic - verify in stock before starting work
- Gasket material selection critical - wrong material causes early failure

---

### Step 7: Pressure Test (55 min)

**Actions:**
1. Close all drain and vent valves
2. Connect pressure test pump to test port on shell side
3. Fill shell side with water (bleed air through vent valve)
4. Close vent valve when water flows (all air purged)
5. Pressurize shell side to 1.5 x design pressure (typically 15-25 bar test pressure)
6. Hold pressure for 30 minutes
7. Inspect for leaks:
   - Header flange joints (both ends)
   - Tube-to-tube sheet joints (inside headers)
   - Shell drain/vent valve packing
8. Monitor pressure gauge (pressure drop >0.5 bar indicates leak)
9. If leaks detected:
   - Depressurize
   - Locate leak source
   - Re-torque bolts if flange leak
   - Plug tube if tube-to-tube sheet leak
   - Retest
10. Repeat test on tube side after shell side passes
11. Document test results on form HX-TEST-001

**Pressure Test Acceptance Criteria:**
- Hold test pressure for 30 minutes
- Pressure drop <0.5 bar over 30 minutes
- No visible leaks at flanges or tubes
- Test pressure = 1.5 x design pressure (verify on heat exchanger nameplate)

**Tools:**
- Hydraulic pressure test pump (0-50 bar range)
- Pressure gauge (calibrated, 0-50 bar)
- Leak detection soap solution (for finding small leaks)
- Pressure test form HX-TEST-001

**Safety:**
⚠️ **CRITICAL:** Stand clear during pressurization (flange failure can eject header)
⚠️ Wear face shield during inspection under pressure
⚠:️ Do not exceed 1.5 x design pressure (risk of equipment damage)

**Expected Result:**
- Pressure test passed on both shell side and tube side
- No leaks detected
- Pressure stable for 30 minutes
- Test documented on form HX-TEST-001

## 6. Final Checkpoints

- [ ] Heat exchanger isolated and depressurized per LOTO
- [ ] Headers removed and stored safely
- [ ] Tube bundle extracted without damage
- [ ] Chemical cleaning completed (tubes clean, no deposits)
- [ ] Cleaning solution neutralized and disposed per regulations
- [ ] Tube bundle reinstalled and aligned
- [ ] Damaged tubes plugged (if any) - <10% total tubes
- [ ] New gaskets installed
- [ ] Header bolts torqued to specification (3 passes, cross pattern)
- [ ] Pressure test passed (shell side and tube side)
- [ ] No leaks detected at test pressure
- [ ] LOTO removed and documented
- [ ] Heat exchanger ready to return to service

## 7. Post-Maintenance Performance Verification

- Monitor heat transfer performance (compare temperatures and flow rates to baseline)
- Check for leaks during first 24 hours of operation
- Verify pressure drops returned to normal levels (indicates effective cleaning)
- Document performance improvement (% increase in heat transfer coefficient)
