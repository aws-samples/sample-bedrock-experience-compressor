# RB-WIND-002: Blade Leading Edge Erosion Repair

**Procedure Code:** PROC-WIND-BLADE-2024-002
**Runbook ID:** RB-WIND-002
**Version:** 2.3
**Date:** 2024-06-25
**Classification:** Corrective Maintenance - Work at Height + Confined Space

## 1. Objective
Repair leading edge erosion damage on wind turbine blades using epoxy coating system to restore aerodynamic profile and prevent further degradation.

## 2. Prerequisites
- Turbine stopped and locked out
- Weather conditions acceptable for rope access work (wind <10 m/s, no precipitation, temperature 10-30°C)
- Rope access team certified (IRATA or equivalent)
- Blade positioned for access (typically 12 o'clock or 6 o'clock position)
- Epoxy materials within shelf life and stored at correct temperature
- Rescue plan reviewed with ground team

## 3. Required Tools
- Rope access equipment (ropes, harnesses, ascenders, descenders)
- Angle grinder with 40-grit disc
- Epoxy repair kit (2-part epoxy, hardener, mixing tools)
- UV cure lamps (if using UV-cure epoxy system)
- Surface preparation tools (scrapers, sandpaper 80/120/220 grit)
- Inspection mirror and flashlight
- Digital camera for documentation
- Weather monitoring equipment

## 4. Estimated Duration
**4 hours per blade** (excludes curing time which depends on ambient temperature)

## 5. Safety Notes
⚠️ **CRITICAL SAFETY WARNINGS:**
- Rope access work - minimum 2-person team with rescue capability
- Weather monitoring mandatory - abort if wind exceeds 10 m/s
- Epoxy materials toxic - wear respirator and chemical-resistant gloves
- UV lamps can cause eye damage - wear UV-blocking safety glasses
- Blade rotation must be locked mechanically (not just brake)
- Emergency descent plan must be established before starting work

---

## STEP-BY-STEP PROCEDURE

### Step 1: Blade Positioning (15 min)

**Actions:**
1. Access turbine SCADA system
2. Manually rotate blade to inspection position (typically 6 o'clock for access from ground)
3. Verify blade pitch angle at 0° (aligned with hub)
4. Apply mechanical lock on pitch system (blade cannot rotate)
5. Apply mechanical lock on yaw system (nacelle cannot rotate)
6. Apply rotor lock (prevents blade rotation around hub)
7. Verify all locks engaged and tagged per LOTO-WIND-002
8. Mark blade number clearly visible from ground (1, 2, or 3)

**Tools:**
- SCADA access terminal
- Mechanical locks (pitch, yaw, rotor)
- LOTO tags
- Marking paint or tape

**Safety:**
⚠️ **CRITICAL:** Blade must be mechanically locked - brake alone insufficient
⚠️ Verify locks engaged before personnel approach blade

**Expected Result:**
- Blade positioned for access (6 o'clock position)
- All mechanical locks engaged and tagged
- Blade clearly marked for identification

---

### Step 2: Leading Edge Inspection (30 min)

**Actions:**
1. Perform ground-level visual inspection with binoculars
2. Identify erosion zones along leading edge
3. Document erosion location (distance from blade root in meters)
4. Estimate erosion severity:
   - Light: surface coating loss, gelcoat damage only
   - Moderate: erosion into laminate, <2mm depth
   - Severe: deep erosion >2mm, structural laminate exposed
5. Deploy rope access team to blade surface for detailed inspection
6. Use inspection mirror to check underside of leading edge
7. Mark erosion boundaries with chalk or tape
8. Photograph damage with scale reference
9. Measure total repair area (length x width)

**Inspection Documentation:**
- Blade number: ___
- Distance from root: ___ meters
- Erosion length: ___ cm
- Maximum erosion depth: ___ mm
- Severity: Light / Moderate / Severe

**Tools:**
- Binoculars (ground inspection)
- Rope access equipment
- Inspection mirror
- Calipers or depth gauge (for erosion measurement)
- Digital camera with scale reference
- Chalk or marking tape

**Safety:**
⚠️ Rope access team must have rescue capability
⚠️ Monitor weather continuously - abort if wind >10 m/s

**Expected Result:**
- Erosion extent documented and photographed
- Repair area clearly marked
- Total surface area calculated (for material estimation)

**Common Issues:**
- Erosion extent often larger than visible from ground - allow extra time for inspection
- Underside of leading edge difficult to inspect - use mirror carefully

---

### Step 3: Surface Preparation (60 min)

**Actions:**
1. Set up rope access work position at erosion zone
2. Remove loose material from eroded area using scraper
3. Grind damaged area with angle grinder (40-grit disc):
   - Remove all loose gelcoat and damaged laminate
   - Create smooth transition from damaged to sound material (taper ratio 1:20)
   - Feather edges to prevent sharp steps
4. Clean surface with solvent (acetone or MEK)
5. Sand surface with progressively finer grits:
   - 80 grit: rough shaping
   - 120 grit: intermediate smoothing
   - 220 grit: final surface prep
6. Vacuum dust from repair area
7. Final cleaning with solvent and lint-free cloth
8. Verify surface dry and oil-free (water break test)

**Surface Preparation Acceptance:**
- All loose material removed
- Smooth taper to sound laminate (no sharp edges)
- Surface clean, dry, and free of contamination
- Ambient temperature 10-30°C (required for epoxy cure)

**Tools:**
- Angle grinder (battery-powered for rope access)
- Grinding discs (40-grit, coarse)
- Sandpaper (80, 120, 220 grit)
- Scrapers and hand tools
- Vacuum cleaner (battery-powered)
- Solvents (acetone, MEK) in squeeze bottles
- Lint-free cleaning cloths

**Safety:**
⚠️ Grinding generates dust - wear respirator (P2 minimum)
⚠️ Power tools on rope create vibration - maintain secure grip
⚠️ Solvents toxic and flammable - use in ventilated area only

**Expected Result:**
- Damaged material removed completely
- Surface prepared for epoxy application
- Clean, dry, contamination-free surface
- Smooth taper to existing blade surface

**Common Issues:**
- Erosion often deeper than initially visible - may expose structural laminate
- Cold weather makes grinding more difficult (material harder)

---

### Step 4: Epoxy Application (90 min)

**Actions:**
1. Verify ambient conditions suitable for epoxy (temperature 10-30°C, humidity <80%)
2. Mix epoxy resin and hardener per manufacturer instructions:
   - Ratio typically 2:1 by weight (verify on product label)
   - Mix thoroughly for 3-5 minutes
   - Pot life typically 30-45 minutes - work quickly
3. Apply first coat (primer coat) with brush:
   - Thin layer to penetrate surface
   - Work into surface texture
   - Allow to tack (5-10 minutes)
4. Apply subsequent coats to build up thickness:
   - Target thickness: match original blade profile
   - Apply in multiple layers (each 1-2mm thick)
   - Allow tacking between coats
5. Shape final coat to match blade aerodynamic profile:
   - Use spatula or squeegee to smooth surface
   - Blend edges into existing blade surface
6. Remove excess material and clean edges
7. If using UV-cure epoxy: position UV lamps and cure per manufacturer specification (typically 30-60 minutes)

**Epoxy Mixing Ratios (verify with product datasheet):**
- Resin: 100 parts by weight
- Hardener: 50 parts by weight (typical 2:1 ratio)
- Total pot life: 30-45 minutes at 20°C
- Tack time: 5-10 minutes per coat

**Tools:**
- Epoxy repair kit (resin, hardener, mixing containers)
- Mixing sticks and scrapers
- Application brushes (disposable)
- Spatulas and squeegees
- UV cure lamps (if applicable)
- Digital scale for measuring ratios
- Thermometer (ambient temperature monitoring)

**Safety:**
⚠️ Epoxy toxic - wear respirator and chemical-resistant gloves
⚠️ UV lamps can damage eyes - wear UV-blocking safety glasses
⚠️ Work in ventilated area - epoxy fumes accumulate in confined space

**Expected Result:**
- Epoxy applied to correct thickness (matches blade profile)
- Smooth finish with no air bubbles or voids
- Good adhesion to prepared surface
- Edges blended smoothly into existing blade

**Common Issues:**
- Epoxy curing temperature sensitive - cold weather extends cure time significantly
- Pot life short in hot weather - work quickly or mix smaller batches
- UV cure requires direct lamp exposure - difficult on curved surfaces

---

### Step 5: Curing Time (60 min minimum, temperature dependent)

**Actions:**
1. Allow epoxy to cure per manufacturer specification
2. Protect repair area from contamination during cure (cover with plastic if rain forecast)
3. Monitor ambient temperature (affects cure time):
   - 10°C: cure time 4-6 hours
   - 20°C: cure time 2-3 hours
   - 30°C: cure time 1-2 hours
4. If using UV cure system: maintain lamp position for specified time (30-60 min typical)
5. Test cure by touch (should be hard, not tacky)
6. Rope access team can descend during cure (return for final inspection)

**Cure Time Estimates:**
- UV cure: 30-60 minutes (lamp-dependent)
- Thermal cure at 20°C: 2-3 hours minimum
- Full cure (maximum strength): 24 hours

**Tools:**
- Thermometer for ambient monitoring
- Plastic sheeting (weather protection if needed)
- UV lamps (if applicable)

**Safety:**
- Personnel may leave work position during cure (if safe to descend)
- Monitor weather during cure period

**Expected Result:**
- Epoxy fully cured (hard, non-tacky surface)
- No contamination during cure
- Ready for final inspection

**Common Issues:**
- Cold weather significantly extends cure time - plan accordingly
- Weather changes during cure (rain, temperature drop) can affect quality

---

### Step 6: Final Inspection (45 min)

**Actions:**
1. Rope access team returns to blade surface
2. Visual inspection of repaired area:
   - Surface smooth and blended with blade profile
   - No air bubbles, voids, or delamination
   - Edges feathered into existing surface
   - Color match acceptable (cosmetic, not critical)
3. Tap test around repair (sound should be solid, not hollow)
4. Measure repair thickness with calipers (should match original blade profile ±1mm)
5. Check adhesion at edges (no lifting or separation)
6. Photograph completed repair for documentation
7. Apply protective coating if specified (optional UV-resistant topcoat)
8. Clean work area (remove all tools and materials from blade)
9. Descend and stow rope access equipment

**Final Inspection Acceptance Criteria:**
- Surface smooth and blended
- No defects (bubbles, voids, delamination)
- Thickness matches blade profile ±1mm
- Good adhesion (no lifting at edges)
- Tap test solid (no hollow sound indicating voids)

**Tools:**
- Calipers (blade profile measurement)
- Tap test tool (coin or small hammer)
- Digital camera for documentation
- Cleaning supplies

**Safety:**
- Verify all tools and materials removed from blade before descent

**Expected Result:**
- Repair meets acceptance criteria
- Blade aerodynamic profile restored
- Documentation complete (photos, measurements)
- Work area cleaned

## 6. Final Checkpoints

- [ ] Blade positioned and mechanically locked
- [ ] Erosion extent documented and photographed
- [ ] Surface prepared (clean, dry, contamination-free)
- [ ] Epoxy mixed and applied per manufacturer specification
- [ ] Cure time adequate for ambient temperature
- [ ] Final inspection passed (smooth surface, good adhesion, correct thickness)
- [ ] Blade profile restored to aerodynamic specification
- [ ] All tools and materials removed from blade
- [ ] LOTO removed and documented
- [ ] Turbine ready to return to service

## 7. Post-Work Monitoring

- Inspect repair after first 100 hours of operation
- Monitor blade balance (vibration analysis) after return to service
- Schedule follow-up inspection at next planned maintenance
