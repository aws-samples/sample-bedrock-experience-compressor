# RB-HYDRO-001: Kaplan Turbine Runner Inspection

**Procedure Code:** PROC-HYDRO-TURB-2024-001
**Runbook ID:** RB-HYDRO-001
**Version:** 3.1
**Date:** 2024-04-15
**Classification:** In-Service Inspection - Confined Space + Underwater Work

## 1. Objective
Annual inspection of Kaplan turbine runner blades and hub to detect cavitation damage, erosion, cracks, and mechanical wear.

## 2. Prerequisites
- Unit shutdown and isolated from penstock
- Cofferdam or gate closure installed upstream
- Dewatering pumps operational and tested
- Confined space entry permit obtained
- Rescue team on standby with retrieval equipment
- Underwater lighting system tested
- Draft tube access scaffolding installed
- UT thickness gauge calibrated (<6 months)

## 3. Required Tools
- UT (Ultrasonic Testing) thickness gauge with underwater capability
- Underwater LED lighting (minimum 5000 lumens)
- Underwater camera (HD video capability)
- Scaffolding components (corrosion-resistant)
- Dewatering pumps (capacity sufficient for site conditions)
- Confined space atmospheric monitor (O2, H2S, CO)
- Silt removal tools (shovels, vacuum pump)
- PPE (waders, waterproof gloves, safety harness, hard hat)

## 4. Estimated Duration
**10 hours** (includes dewatering and re-flooding time)

## 5. Safety Notes
⚠️ **CRITICAL SAFETY WARNINGS:**
- Confined space with potential for oxygen deficiency and toxic gases
- Drowning hazard - verify all gates closed and locked before dewatering
- Slip and fall hazard - surfaces covered in silt and algae
- Heavy equipment overhead during inspection (runner mass typically 15-30 tonnes)
- Emergency rescue plan must be in place before entry
- Continuous atmospheric monitoring required
- Do not enter confined space alone - minimum 2-person team plus attendant

## STEP-BY-STEP PROCEDURE

### Step 1: Dewatering and Cofferdam (120 min)

**Actions:**
1. Verify unit electrically and mechanically isolated per LOTO-HYDRO-001
2. Close upstream penstock gate (wicket gates already closed from unit shutdown)
3. Verify gate closure complete (zero water flow through turbine)
4. Install temporary cofferdam or bulkhead (if not permanent installation)
5. Position dewatering pumps at draft tube sump (lowest point)
6. Begin dewatering operation:
   - Initial phase: high water volume (expect 500-2000m³ depending on draft tube size)
   - Monitor pump performance (watch for cavitation or loss of prime)
   - Typical dewatering time: 90-120 minutes
7. Monitor water level during dewatering (mark levels every 30 minutes)
8. When water level drops below runner blade tips, reduce pump flow (prevent dry-running)
9. Continue pumping until runner fully exposed
10. Maintain continuous dewatering (seepage through gates typically 5-20 L/min)

**Dewatering Monitoring:**
- Initial water level: ___ meters above runner
- Pump capacity: ___ m³/hour
- Estimated dewatering time: ___ minutes
- Seepage rate after dewatering: ___ L/min

**Tools:**
- Dewatering pumps (diesel or electric, 100-500 m³/hour capacity)
- Discharge hoses (large diameter)
- Water level measuring tape
- Cofferdam components (if temporary installation)

**Safety:**
⚠️ **CRITICAL:** Verify gates locked closed before dewatering begins
⚠️ Pump discharge area must handle high flow rates (prevent flooding adjacent areas)

**Expected Result:**
- Draft tube dewatered completely
- Runner blades and hub fully exposed
- Continuous dewatering pumps operational (managing seepage)
- Safe to install scaffolding and access equipment

**Common Issues:**
- Silt accumulation in draft tube increases dewatering time significantly
- Gate seepage higher than expected - may require larger dewatering capacity
- Pump intake screens clog with debris - monitor and clean regularly

---

### Step 2: Runner Access (45 min)

**Actions:**
1. Perform atmospheric test at draft tube entrance:
   - Oxygen: 19.5-23.5% (acceptable)
   - H2S: <10 ppm (acceptable)
   - CO: <35 ppm (acceptable)
   - Combustible gases: <10% LEL (acceptable)
2. Install ventilation system (forced air circulation, minimum 4 air changes per hour)
3. Continuous atmospheric monitoring (mount detector at runner level)
4. Install scaffolding from draft tube floor to runner level (typically 3-5 meter height)
5. Install safety railings on scaffold platforms
6. Install ladder access to scaffold
7. Load test scaffolding (static load 500 kg for 5 minutes)
8. Position underwater lighting around runner (LED panels, waterproof to IP68)
9. Set up decontamination area (wash station at draft tube entrance)

**Atmospheric Acceptance Criteria:**
- Oxygen: 19.5-23.5%
- H2S: <10 ppm
- CO: <35 ppm
- Combustible gases: <10% LEL

**Tools:**
- Atmospheric monitor (4-gas detector minimum: O2, H2S, CO, LEL)
- Scaffolding components (corrosion-resistant, certified)
- Ventilation blower (explosion-proof)
- Underwater LED lighting (IP68 rated)
- Fall protection equipment

**Safety:**
⚠️ **CRITICAL:** Do not enter confined space until atmosphere verified safe
⚠️ Continuous monitoring required - detector must alarm if conditions change
⚠️ Ventilation must run continuously during all work
⚠️ Attendant must remain outside draft tube entrance at all times

**Expected Result:**
- Atmosphere safe for entry
- Scaffolding installed and load-tested
- Lighting positioned for inspection
- Emergency rescue equipment staged at entrance

---

### Step 3: Blade Visual Inspection (60 min)

**Actions:**
1. Entry team dons PPE (waders, waterproof gloves, harness, hard hat)
2. Team enters draft tube (2 persons: inspector + safety watch)
3. Climb scaffold to runner level
4. Perform general visual survey of all runner blades (typically 4-6 blades)
5. Look for damage types:
   - Cavitation damage (pitted surface, material loss on blade suction side)
   - Erosion (leading edge wear from sediment abrasion)
   - Cracks (especially at blade root and hub attachment)
   - Corrosion (oxidation, pitting)
   - Mechanical damage (impact marks, deformation)
6. Mark damaged areas with waterproof chalk or grease pencil
7. Photograph each blade (use scale reference in photos)
8. Document blade condition on inspection form HYDRO-BLADE-001

**Cavitation Damage Characteristics:**
- Location: typically on blade suction side (low pressure surface)
- Appearance: rough, pitted surface with material loss
- Severity levels:
  - Light: surface roughness, no measurable depth
  - Moderate: pitting 1-3mm deep
  - Severe: pitting >3mm deep, may expose weld seams or internal structure

**Erosion Characteristics:**
- Location: blade leading edges (sediment impact zone)
- Appearance: smooth material loss, thinning of blade profile
- Measurement: compare blade thickness to original drawings

**Tools:**
- Waterproof chalk or grease pencil
- Digital camera (waterproof or in protective housing)
- Calibrated scale (for photo reference)
- Flashlight (LED, waterproof)
- Inspection form HYDRO-BLADE-001

**Safety:**
⚠️ Surfaces slippery (algae, silt residue) - use caution when moving on scaffold
⚠️ Monitor atmospheric conditions continuously
⚠️ Maximum 30 minutes inside confined space - rotate personnel

**Expected Result:**
- All blades visually inspected
- Damage areas marked and photographed
- Condition documented for trending

---

### Step 4: UT Thickness Measurement (90 min)

**Actions:**
1. Select measurement locations on each blade:
   - Leading edge (sediment erosion zone)
   - Suction side (cavitation zone)
   - Pressure side (baseline reference)
   - Trailing edge
   - Blade root area (high stress)
2. Clean measurement surfaces with wire brush (remove algae, scale, loose material)
3. Apply couplant gel to UT probe (improves acoustic contact)
4. Measure thickness at marked locations:
   - Take 3 readings per location (average for accuracy)
   - Record minimum thickness reading
5. Compare measurements to:
   - Original blade thickness (from manufacturing drawings)
   - Previous inspection data (trending)
6. Calculate material loss percentage
7. Identify areas with >10% material loss (requires engineering review)
8. Mark areas with >20% material loss (may require weld repair)
9. Document measurements on form HYDRO-UT-001

**UT Measurement Acceptance Criteria:**
- Blade nominal thickness: varies by location (typically 25-50mm)
- Acceptable material loss: <10% of nominal thickness
- Engineering review required: 10-20% material loss
- Repair required: >20% material loss

**UT Measurement Locations (typical grid):**
```
For each blade (4-6 blades total):
- Leading edge: 5 points (hub to tip)
- Mid-chord suction side: 5 points
- Mid-chord pressure side: 5 points
- Trailing edge: 5 points
- Blade root: 8 points (high stress area)
Total: ~28 measurements per blade
```

**Tools:**
- UT thickness gauge (capable of underwater use or with surface dry enough for standard gauge)
- Couplant gel
- Wire brush (for surface preparation)
- Thickness measurement form HYDRO-UT-001
- Previous inspection data (for comparison)

**Safety:**
- Rotate personnel every 30 minutes (confined space fatigue)
- Monitor atmospheric conditions continuously

**Expected Result:**
- Thickness measurements completed at all specified locations
- Material loss quantified and compared to baseline
- Areas requiring repair identified
- Data documented for trending

**Common Issues:**
- UT calibration drift in high humidity environment - verify calibration periodically
- Surface preparation critical for accurate readings - may take longer than planned
- Coupling difficult on rough surfaces (cavitation damage) - multiple readings needed

### Step 5: Cavitation Damage Assessment (75 min)

**Actions:**
1. Focus on areas with visible cavitation (identified in Step 3)
2. Measure cavitation depth using depth gauge or calipers
3. Map cavitation extent (length x width of affected area)
4. Classify cavitation severity:
   - Class 1: Surface roughness, <1mm depth (acceptable, monitor)
   - Class 2: Pitting 1-3mm depth (plan repair next outage)
   - Class 3: Pitting >3mm depth (repair this outage)
5. Photograph cavitation damage with measurement scale
6. Document cavitation location on blade diagram
7. Correlate cavitation patterns with operating conditions:
   - High cavitation near leading edge = high angle of attack operation
   - Suction side cavitation = low tailwater level or high flow rate
8. Recommend operating parameter adjustments if cavitation excessive

**Cavitation Damage Documentation:**
- Blade number: ___
- Location on blade: ___ (leading/mid/trailing, hub/mid/tip)
- Affected area: ___ cm x ___ cm
- Maximum pit depth: ___ mm
- Severity class: 1 / 2 / 3
- Repair recommendation: Monitor / Plan repair / Immediate repair

**Tools:**
- Depth gauge or calipers (0-50mm range)
- Ruler or measuring tape (for area mapping)
- Digital camera with scale reference
- Blade diagram (for marking cavitation locations)

**Safety:**
- Cavitation damage creates sharp edges - wear cut-resistant gloves

**Expected Result:**
- Cavitation damage quantified (depth, area, severity)
- Repair priorities established
- Operating parameter recommendations documented

---

### Step 6: Blade Hub Inspection (60 min)

**Actions:**
1. Inspect blade hub (central structure connecting blades to shaft)
2. Check blade attachment mechanism:
   - For adjustable-blade runners: inspect pivot pins, bearings, seals
   - For fixed-blade runners: inspect weld seams at blade root
3. Look for cracks in hub structure:
   - Visual inspection with magnifying glass
   - Dye penetrant testing (PT) on suspect areas
4. Check hub surface for cavitation or erosion
5. Inspect blade pitch adjustment mechanism (if applicable):
   - Linkage arms (corrosion, wear, play in joints)
   - Servomotor connection
   - Position indicators
6. Measure blade pitch angle (if adjustable) at each blade:
   - Compare to specification
   - Verify all blades at same angle (synchronization)
7. Test blade pitch adjustment mechanism (if safe to do so):
   - Apply test pressure to servomotor
   - Verify blade movement smooth (no binding)
8. Photograph hub and attachment components

**Hub Inspection Focus Areas:**
- Blade root attachment (highest stress concentration)
- Weld seams (crack initiation sites)
- Bearing surfaces (wear, corrosion)
- Seals (leakage, deterioration)

**Tools:**
- Magnifying glass or borescope
- Dye penetrant test kit (for crack detection)
- Angle measurement tool (protractor or digital level)
- Calipers (for wear measurement)
- Digital camera

**Safety:**
⚠️ Do not operate pitch adjustment mechanism if personnel near moving blades

**Expected Result:**
- Hub structure inspected (no cracks or excessive wear)
- Blade attachment secure
- Pitch adjustment mechanism operational (if applicable)
- All findings documented

---

### Step 7: Photo Documentation (30 min)

**Actions:**
1. Systematic photographic documentation of entire runner:
   - Each blade (full view)
   - Each area with damage (close-up with scale)
   - Hub structure
   - Blade attachments
   - Overall runner assembly
2. Video recording of complete inspection (narrated walkthrough)
3. Organize photos by blade number and damage type
4. Upload photos to inspection database
5. Prepare preliminary inspection report summary

**Photo Documentation Requirements:**
- Minimum 20 photos per inspection (more if significant damage)
- Include calibrated scale in all damage photos
- Label photos with: date, unit ID, blade number, location
- Store in permanent inspection archive

**Tools:**
- Digital camera (waterproof or in protective housing)
- Video camera
- Calibrated scale (for reference)
- Photo labeling software or forms

**Safety:**
- No specific hazards during documentation phase

**Expected Result:**
- Complete photographic record of inspection
- Photos organized and archived
- Video recording completed

---

### Step 8: Re-flooding (120 min)

**Actions:**
1. Perform final walkthrough (verify no tools or materials left in draft tube)
2. Remove atmospheric monitoring equipment
3. Remove scaffolding and lighting equipment
4. Remove personnel from draft tube
5. Close draft tube access hatch
6. Stop dewatering pumps
7. Open drain valve or vent to allow controlled re-flooding:
   - Slow re-flooding prevents damage to runner from water hammer
   - Typical re-flooding time: 90-120 minutes
8. Monitor water level during re-flooding
9. When draft tube fully flooded, open wicket gates slightly to equalize pressure
10. Verify no abnormal leakage or damage from re-flooding process
11. Remove cofferdam or bulkhead (if temporary)
12. Perform post-inspection equipment check (all tools accounted for)

**Re-flooding Precautions:**
- Re-flood slowly (prevent water hammer damage to runner or draft tube)
- Verify all personnel clear before re-flooding begins
- Monitor for abnormal water flow or leakage

**Tools:**
- Water level monitoring equipment
- Tool inventory checklist

**Safety:**
⚠️ **CRITICAL:** Verify all personnel out of draft tube before re-flooding
⚠️ No entry into draft tube during re-flooding

**Expected Result:**
- Draft tube re-flooded safely
- All equipment removed and accounted for
- Unit ready for return to service (pending inspection report review)

## 6. Final Checkpoints

- [ ] Dewatering completed (runner fully exposed)
- [ ] Atmospheric monitoring passed (O2, H2S, CO within limits)
- [ ] All blades visually inspected (damage marked and photographed)
- [ ] UT thickness measurements completed (material loss quantified)
- [ ] Cavitation damage assessed and classified
- [ ] Hub and blade attachments inspected
- [ ] Photo documentation completed
- [ ] No tools or materials left in draft tube (FOD check)
- [ ] Draft tube re-flooded safely
- [ ] Inspection forms completed (HYDRO-BLADE-001, HYDRO-UT-001)
- [ ] Preliminary inspection report prepared

## 7. Post-Inspection Actions

- Engineering review of inspection data (within 48 hours)
- Determine repair requirements and schedule
- Update turbine maintenance history
- Trending analysis (compare to previous inspections)
- Operating parameter recommendations (if cavitation excessive)
